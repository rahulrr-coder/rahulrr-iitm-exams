Execution protocol · four papers · one rotation per pass

Three passes to Sunday

One rotation through all four subjects per pass. Core only — the tail stays shut. Every pass goes deeper on the same material rather than wider onto new material. Exploration ends Thursday; Friday and Saturday are revision with nothing new opened.

Pass 1 · Clear the core

Learn the pattern with the answer key open. You are not testing yourself yet, you are building recognition. Finish a subject completely before moving to the next.

Pass 2 · Must-solve PYQs

Same rotation, same core archetypes — now the flagged must-solve questions, cold, key closed. Every miss goes in the error log with why you went wrong, not just the right answer.

Pass 3 · Good-to-solve sweep

The second tier of questions on core archetypes, same rotation. This is the first pass you are allowed to abandon mid-subject — a Pass 2 miss always outranks a Pass 3 fresh question.

Why this order

English first — the cheapest recognition material, and it starts the daily passage habit. CT second because it carries 1100 marks at the best marks-per-hour in the exam, so it has to clear before time gets tight. Math 2 sits last deliberately: worst marks-per-hour in the paper set, and the designated sacrifice if the gate arrives before the rotation finishes.

The gate · Thursday 10 September, end of day

No new material after this line

Nothing new gets opened after Thursday. No new archetype, no new question type, no tail, no “quick look” at a topic you skipped.

  • Allowed: re-doing ticked archetypes, error-log redo, timed papers with keys, formula recall from memory, English passages.
  • Not allowed: any archetype you have never seen, the core tail, rebuilding this plan.
  • Saturday stops at 6pm. Bag packed, alarm set, sleep by 10:30.

Locked · the core tail

Opens only when all three passes are done for all four subjects. That will not happen this term, and that is the plan working rather than failing.

The tail is 205 archetypes averaging 4–11 marks each, against a core averaging 10–28. A shaky core archetype has roughly twenty times more marks riding on it than a perfect tail one. Second pass on core always beats first pass on tail.

True every single day

  • One English comprehension passage, ten minutes. Including grind days, including the gate day. 295 marks depend on a skill that cannot be crammed on the 12th.
  • Ten minutes of recognition review. Collocations, modals, idioms, prepositions, CT dictionary properties. This material decays — read once and it is gone by the 13th.
  • Error log before you close the session. Question, your answer, correct answer, why you went wrong. A miss you do not diagnose is a miss you repeat.
  • Match the work to the day. WFH days take execution — recursion traces, DFS, updateMatrix, gradients, Hessian. College days take recognition, which survives being done in fragments.
  • MSQ discipline: evaluate every option independently, never stop at the first correct one. SA discipline: arithmetic on paper, obey the rounding instruction exactly.

One timed paper, cold, by Tuesday

42 papers with answer keys are sitting unused. Recognising an archetype with the key open and producing it in the hall are different skills, and the gap between them is where marks actually die. Tuesday leaves four days to act on whatever it tells you.
